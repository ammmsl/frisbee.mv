How does UFA's retention compare to parkrun, gyms and clubs?

Part A's strongest retention benchmarks: parkrun's 22%-once / 76%-few-timers / 4.3%-weekly split, gym cliffs of 47%/63%/81% dropout at 3/6/12 months, ~66% gym annual retention, and the 10–30% event-turnout band. Findings
  • The front door is the leak, exactly as the periphery reports say: 50% of 307 newcomers played exactly once and 72% five times or fewer. This all-newcomer figure sits below SOC-4/CB-A's ~81–87% because those pages count only the periphery who never joined the roster, whereas this cohort also includes the newcomers who converted to regulars (median ~7 plays). It is a denominator difference, not a substrate one — SOC-4/CB-A are now re-analysed in full (2026-07-11) on the same Actual-Attendees presence substrate this page uses, and periphery repeat-play barely moves (13% vs 14% played 2+ on the old first-timer log). Both figures are correct at their own denominator.
  • Against gyms, UFA trades a harder entry for a stickier core: survival is 37% at 3 months (gyms ~53%) but 30% at 12 (gyms ~19%). That is the parkrun pattern from Part A — free/cheap entry produces front-loaded attrition, and whoever converts becomes far more durable than a gym member.
  • The habit-dose threshold replicates behaviourally: newcomers reaching ≥4 sessions in their first 6 weeks survive to 6 months at 64% vs 24% below the dose (n=52/207) — the Kaushal & Rhodes gym-habit dose, visible in pickup data. Selection and causation are confounded (keen people both attend early and stay), so this is a screening marker, not a lever, until tested.
  • Membership renewal is strong: 37 of 51 first-year members renewed (72%) plus 19 new joiners — against the ~66% commercial-gym annual retention benchmark, and despite the fee dropping 700→250 (the renewal decision was made at the cheaper price; see OMS-13).
  • Turnout runs at the top of the engaged band: a median 24% of the trailing-90-day pool shows up to any given session, twice a week — versus the 10–30% single-event heuristic for engaged communities. On cadence UFA is already at Part A's 'natural rhythm' (weekly-plus), which the habit literature says is the regime where routines consolidate.
Method, data & caveats
Cohort = persons first seen after 2024-02-01 in the presence substrate (gS_rsvp.presence(): Session Input "Actual Attendees" resolved at 99.4%, deduped, 307 people) — the opening 2024 wave is excluded as left-truncated incumbents. Survival beyond m months = last presence ≥ first presence + m months, computed only over people with ≥m months of possible follow-up (curve stops when n<40). Being "alive" is retrospective (a later return revives a person), which slightly flatters the tail vs a gym-contract definition — the gym comparison is directional. Engagement categories are total session counts (once / ≤5 / ≥20) against parkrun's published once/few/high categories (different windows; shape comparison, not a like-for-like rate). Dose = ≥4 presences within 42 days of first session (Kaushal & Rhodes: 4×/week × 6 weeks in gyms; pickup caps at 2/week, so the analogue is ~a third of their dose). Turnout = monthly mean real_pax ÷ distinct players in the trailing 90 days. Renewal from member_2025/member_2026 flags. All aggregates, dedup applied, no names. Script analysis/gP_c2.py. [Interpretation limitation, internally verified] A portion of the newcomers who play exactly once are short-stay visitors — travelling players and novelty drop-ins who were never convertible — so the count of genuine conversion failures is smaller than the one-and-done share implies. The adjustment falls on the count, not the rate (visitors leave after one play at about the base rate). The basis is a decision-grade internal owner-coding worksheet; no figure from it is published here.
