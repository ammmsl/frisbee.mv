Findings
  • After correcting for overdispersion, participation shows no distinguishable gap: women check in at a rate ratio of 0.771 vs men (Negative-Binomial 95% CI 0.579–1.028, p=0.076). The point estimate leans lower for women, but the interval includes parity.
  • The naive Poisson model would have lied: it reports IRR 0.714 with a tight CI [0.667, 0.764], but deviance/df = 19.1 (≫1) means the counts are far more variable than Poisson allows. The honest CI is roughly 4× wider — a small-n modelling caution, not a gender result.
  • Centrality tilts male on the median (@-in-degree 9 for men vs 4 for women, Mann–Whitney p=0.062), but the distributions overlap heavily — a lean, not a wall.
  • [ASSUMPTION] Gender is roster-only and binary as recorded; 178 people are excluded. Exposure = days from first_activity to last_attended (floored at 30). No causal claim — this is who plays and who is named, not why.
Method, data & caveats
Poisson GLM (statsmodels) of attendance_count ~ gender with an offset = log(exposure days), exposure = first_activity→last_attended (floored at 30 days). The Poisson fit is badly overdispersed (deviance/df = 19.1), so we refit a Negative-Binomial (NB2) — reported as the honest model (estimated α=0.7); the rate ratio is exp(β_female) with its Wald 95% CI. Centrality = weighted @-mention in-degree and eigenvector on the roster nodes present in the graph; gender difference tested with Mann–Whitney.
