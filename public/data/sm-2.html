Findings
  • The actual administrative bureaucracy is near-monopolised: Gini of membership-event actions = 0.948 (95% CI 0.895–0.965) — far above the play Gini of 0.669 (CI 0.626–0.702), with no CI overlap.
  • The broader composite proxy (actions + @-attention + messages) is Gini 0.69 (CI 0.63–0.729) — only marginally above play and with overlapping CIs. Visibility is shared more widely than the paperwork is.
  • Only 28 of 136 people have ever performed a tracked membership action, and one person accounts for 83 of 225 — a textbook volunteer-labour bottleneck. Top 10% of the composite proxy carry 55%.
  • [ASSUMPTION] Equal weights on the three composite signals (no ground truth to calibrate). The near-monopoly finding rests on the raw action count, which needs no weighting.
Method, data & caveats
Admin-load proxy = sum of three min-max-scaled signals over the 136 @-mention-network nodes: (1) membership_events actor count, (2) weighted @-mention in-degree, (3) message count. Each scaled to [0,1] so no single signal dominates by unit; equal-weighted sum ∈ [0,3]. Play = people.attendance_count over the 172 people with ≥1 check-in. Gini via the standard sorted-cumulative formula; 95% CIs are percentile bootstraps (2,000 resamples). Lorenz curve = cumulative population share vs cumulative value share. This is descriptive concentration, not a causal claim about effort. Substrate analysis/gA_graph.py; script analysis/gA_sm2.py.
