With the org-wide AGM 2025 books in hand, the fee cut cannot be a solvency move: the association closed 2025 debt-free with a ~Rf 19,566 (US$1,271) surplus, and the field booking that drives cost is a pass-through (income ≈ expense ≈ Rf 63,425 (US$4,119)) that the membership fee neither funds nor tracks. The surplus comes from membership + sponsorship, not from pricing play. So 700→350 is a demand-side rationing / patronage lever set by governance: a flat 700 over-rationed the low-attendance member; the cut widens the entry gate while the non-member surcharge rises (15→25 MVR/session), moving the load from the joining price to per-use.

Findings
  • The 700→350 cut (50%) is not financial necessity: the org closed 2025 debt-free with a ~Rf 19,566 (US$1,271) surplus, and the cost driver — field booking — is a pass-through (Rf 63,425 (US$4,119), income≈expense) the fee never funded. Solvency is not the reason the price moved.
  • It is a demand-side rationing lever. A flat 700 over-rationed the low-attendance member — a steep entry price for a few sessions a year suppresses casual access. Halving it widens the gate to exactly those members, a deliberate access choice by the AGM.
  • The load shifted from the joining price to per-use: as the fee fell, the non-member surcharge rose (15→25 MVR/session) and a member surcharge appeared (0→10). UFA is rationing on use, not on entry — cheaper to belong, more to pay-as-you-play as a non-member.
  • The fee is decoupled from cost in direction: per-player field cost rose ~34→~46 MVR/session while the fee was cut. A cost-recovery fee would have risen with the line; UFA moved it the opposite way — confirming it is a signal about who to nudge in, not a price of the resource.
  • [ASSUMPTION] 'Over-rationing' is inferred from the flat-fee structure + the attendance distribution (most members play few sessions; see OMS-6), not from a stated demand curve. The reading is that the fee is a governance-set access lever, softening OMS-12's runway framing: with a debt-free surplus, the cut is affordable by design, not forced.
