Findings
  • The AGM 2025 org-wide picture is solvent and debt-free: income Rf 165,475 (US$10,745) vs expense Rf 145,909 (US$9,475), a Rf 19,566 (US$1,271) surplus, Rf 159,570 (US$10,362) collected and only Rf 5,567 (US$362) owed. This is the authoritative whole-of-org view, distinct from the pickup slice.
  • Field booking is a pass-through: Rf 63,425 (US$4,119) appears on both income and expense (members pay in for turf; UFA pays it straight out to the venue). So the pickup field-booking slice is ~cost-neutral — the ~Rf 145,600 that OMS-6/12 trace is money passing through, not surplus.
  • The surplus is therefore carried by membership dues (Rf 34,800 (US$2,260)) + sponsorship (Rf 26,100 (US$1,695)), not by pricing play. This is why the fee can be a patronage/access lever (OMS-6, OMS-13) rather than a cost-recovery number — play pays for itself; membership and sponsors fund the rest.
  • This softens OMS-12's runway alarm: a two-week treasury outage is a bookkeeping and bus-factor exposure on the pickup slice, not a solvency or runway risk. On the org-wide books the association is solvent and debt-free — the 'runway depletion' framing was a slice artifact.
  • Prior years — subsidy → pass-through → surcharge-led. The DB pickup slice shows the model shifting: in 2024 players were charged Rf 35,700 (US$2,318) against a Rf 56,700 (US$3,682) field cost, so UFA subsidised ~Rf 21,000 (US$1,364) of pickup (no surcharge). By 2025 charging caught up to field cost — the cost-neutral pass-through this report describes — and the surcharge appeared (Rf 7,560 (US$491)). In 2026-H1 the surcharge is already Rf 13,760 (US$894) (~30% of charges). This is the multi-year face of OMS-13's rationing lever: the load moved from a flat entry fee onto per-use.
  • Collection discipline is consistently high; the 2026 open balance is timing. Fully-settled sessions were 99.3% of charges in 2024 and 96.8% in 2025 — tight. The lower 75.9% in 2026-H1 (Rf 10,888 (US$707) outstanding) is recency — recent sessions still being settled — mirroring the small Rf 5,567 (US$362) receivable on the 2025 org-wide books. It is a lag, not slippage.
  • [ASSUMPTION] These are INTERNAL category aggregates, reconciled to the 2025 financial-records workbook and summing exactly to the attested AGM totals — coarse buckets, no named counterparties and no residual/balancing line. The figures are pre-audit: a formal external audit is staged to National-Association status by design (the annual Ministry submission already sets the financial-year reporting cycle); sub-category precision below these buckets is not claimed.
Method, data & caveats
What this is. The org-wide finance companion to OMS-6 (fee coherence) and OMS-12 (buffer stress test), which analyse only the pickup field-booking slice in ufa.sqlite (~Rf 145,600). Here we present the AGM 2025 org-wide picture from gE_events.FIN25 — income Rf 165,475 (US$10,745), expense Rf 145,909 (US$9,475), surplus Rf 19,566 (US$1,271), collected Rf 159,570 (US$10,362), owed Rf 5,567 (US$362), debt-free — with income/expense composition from gE_events.FIN25_INCOME / FIN25_EXPENSE. The analytic bridge is the field pass-through (Rf 63,425 (US$4,119)), identical on both sides, which makes the pickup slice cost-neutral and locates the surplus in membership + sponsorship. Sources: the AGM 2025 financial report, cross-checked and reconciled to the INTERNAL "Financial Records 2025" workbook — category aggregates only, summing exactly to the attested totals, with no residual line. Money at ~15.4 MVR/US$. No counterparties named — no person, sponsor, or vendor; any line resolving to one is merged up. Descriptive aggregate, not an audit.
