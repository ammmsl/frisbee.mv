Findings
  • The canonical structure shows up uninvited: a community that never read Dunbar reproduces 5/15/50/(150–204) almost exactly, sits at the top of the 10–30% turnout band, fires sessions at precisely its declared quorum, and matches the ~10% active-core rule. The breakpoints literature, borrowed from congregations and phone logs, describes a Malé frisbee pitch startlingly well.
  • Where UFA deviates, it deviates instructively: no-shows at the paid band's floor and a 1.00× overbooking multiplier (the socially-curated list beats the RSVP-decay assumption); a steeper newcomer cliff than gyms but a stickier tail (the parkrun trade, at club scale); 90-9-1 flattened at the top because a body can only fill one slot per night.
  • The one clear structural strain: the volunteer economy is thin — 8.5:1 players per admin volunteer, ~41:1 against the ~5-person real core. Every other deviation is neutral or favourable; this one is the club's structural risk (the same finding as TB-5/OMS-4). We cite no national "benchmark" ratio here: England-wide volunteering figures mix definitions of participant and volunteer and aren't measured at club level, so any single headline ratio would mislead more than clarify.
  • Two literature gaps are filled outright (no-show rates, attendance distributions), one is behaviourally measured for the first time (lapsed-player outcomes), one gets its first real-world case (a league below the 6–8 floor). Gap #3 stays open and is labelled so. This page is the anchor for the website-published resource (Part B plan §5).
