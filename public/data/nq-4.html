Bridge or separate clique?
Findings
  • The Hulhumalé HDC futsal venue bridges the community, not splits it: 30 of 31 HDC players (97%) also play the Villingili core, and only 1 plays at HDC exclusively. The second venue is the same core on a smaller pitch, not a breakaway group.
  • There is no separate “format” to bridge or split — the 4 HDC sessions are the same pickup game at a futsal-sized pitch, split across 2 different category tags in the DB. On Feld's logic a shared new focus bridges only if it mixes otherwise-separate people; here it re-mixes insiders.
  • Structurally the club is one co-attending community around Villingili (218 sessions): every satellite venue — FAM Malé, the two other Hulhumalé pitches, Henveiru — draws ≈100% from the same core. There is no venue-defined sub-community.
  • [ASSUMPTION] HDC futsal has only 4 recorded sessions, so this is a direction, not a rate — no null test is meaningful at this n. Re-run once the Mon/Thu HDC slot has more sessions to confirm it keeps drawing the core rather than recruiting a separate futsal crowd.
Method, data & caveats
Bipartite people×venue graph from attendance ⋈ sessions, projected onto the venue side (edge weight = shared attendees; overlap also by Jaccard). Venues are normalised from the messy location strings (analysis/corrections.py). Bridging = share of a venue's attendees who also play the Villingili core (integration) vs the share exclusive to it (separation). We deliberately do not facet by the DB's session category — it is a venue artifact (CORRECTIONS.md §1), not a behavioural category. n=172 people with per-session rows; the HDC futsal venue has only 4 sessions, so this is descriptive early signal, not an estimate.
