Why this is a commons at all. UFA's play depends on a rivalrous, rented, shared resource — turf-time on a single FAM pitch (Villingili), booked and paid collectively (a pass-through, income≈expense). That is a textbook small common-pool resource: the field is finite, one group's booking excludes another's, and no member owns it. So Ostrom's design principles for enduring commons are the right lens for asking whether the shared turf is well-governed — not whether the club is profitable.

Findings
  • UFA is a genuinely-recognised, well-nested commons: it scores met on P7 (recognition — COS registration, Ministry & Council correspondence, WFDF Provisional NF) and P8 (nesting — WFDF above, City Councils alongside, portfolio committees below). These are exactly the principles young commons most often lack, and UFA secured them early.
  • Collective choice (P3) is real: an AGM elects the committee by STV and sets the rules (fees, surcharge) — the people under the rules make them. Rules also fit local conditions (P2): bulk-prepaid turf, a member/non-member surcharge, and hat formats sized to turnout all track the actual field cost and cadence.
  • The gap is the interior enforcement machinery. Monitoring (P4) is partial — money is ledgered but on a single role (bus-factor 1), and field-rule compliance is unmonitored beyond players watching players. Graduated sanctions (P5) are weak: the only formal sanction is blunt Ethics-Committee discipline, with no escalating ladder for no-shows or unpaid dues.
  • UFA substitutes norms for enforcement: 'spirit of the game' self-officiating resolves in-play calls at near-zero cost (a P6 strength) and the flat surcharge is a price, not a penalty. This works at 56 members and one pitch, but it means the commons is held together by goodwill, not graduated sanction — the classic fragility as it scales.
  • [ASSUMPTION] Statuses are the run's document-coded reading on a met/partial/weak scale (11/16 overall), not a validated Ostrom-compliance instrument. They express where governance is thick vs thin, not a certification. The boundary (P1) score in particular hinges on treating turf-time, not membership, as the appropriated resource.
Method, data & caveats
Framework. Elinor Ostrom's eight design principles for enduring common-pool-resource institutions (Governing the Commons, 1990): (1) clearly-defined boundaries, (2) congruence between rules and local conditions, (3) collective-choice arrangements, (4) monitoring, (5) graduated sanctions, (6) low-cost conflict-resolution mechanisms, (7) minimal recognition of the right to organise, (8) nested enterprises. The common-pool resource is shared turf-time on a single rented FAM pitch (Villingili) — finite, rivalrous, collectively booked (a pass-through). Each principle is scored on a three-level scale — met (2) / partial (1) / weak (0) — coloured with the design system's semantic tokens; overall = 11/16. Sources: governance corpus (gD_evidence, PUBLIC + INTERNAL-aggregate) — Articles of Association, AGM 2024/2025 records, WFDF NF application, Ministry/Council correspondence — plus the event/finance reality (gE_events: field pass-through, surcharge, the 17-May debrief).
