Do people show up when they say they will?

Findings
  • Gap #1 filled — the first measured pickup no-show rates we know of: across 171 paid sessions and 4,246 commitments, 16.2% of people who ever signed a list did not play (any-point), and 9.2% of the final pre-start list did not show. Part A's practitioner bands put paid events at 15–30% and free RSVPs at 40–50%: this community operates at the paid band's floor, one-third of free-event norms.
  • The list is a nearly unbiased headcount instrument: walk-ons (12.6%) roughly offset no-shows, so the final list predicts headcount at a median 1.00× (p90 1.08×). The literature's 1.5–2× overbooking heuristic is unnecessary here — a finding in itself: paid, identity-visible, socially-curated RSVP eliminates the overbooking problem.
  • Commitment front-loads: lists go out a median 27h ahead and hit the 12-player quorum within ~8h — the cascade is steep early then plateaus, matching Granovetter's threshold model with a low-threshold core (the regulars who sign first) triggering the rest. Explicit partial commitments ("50/50") are rare (11 across all final lists) — the list culture forces a binary.
  • Stability across a price shock: no-show rates were flat-to-improving over 2024→2026 (16.0% → 14.1% any-point) — the 700→250 patronage restructure did not loosen commitment discipline (descriptive, not causal; see NQ-3).
  • Membership status is the strongest reliability marker in the data: members break final-list commitments at 4.4% vs 21.6% for non-members — a ~5× gap. This is selection, not a price effect (the committed core self-selects into membership, and unresolved one-off guests count as non-members), but it means the member roll and the reliable core are nearly the same people — the headcount a booking can actually be planned on is, to first order, the members plus a discounted guest count.
  • Individual Granovetter thresholds are measurable, and the distribution is exactly his shape: of 115 regulars, only 8 are habitual early-committers (median signup position ≤6) while 67 typically join after the 12-player quorum is met. The tiny low-threshold core that starts every cascade is also its most reliable segment (97% vs 85% attendance on commitments) — remove those ~8 people and the nightly cascade has no ignition. Part A's threshold model, previously a thought experiment, gets its first person-level empirical distribution here.
  • Method caveat: rates are computed on dated Villingili lists only (n=172; name-resolution 95.7%). Unresolved names compare as normalised strings, so residual spelling drift can create offsetting phantom no-show/walk-on pairs — the true rates are, if anything, slightly lower.
Method, data & caveats
Substrate analysis/gS_rsvp.py: sign-up lists parsed from the chat, assigned to sessions by header date, names resolved via the aliases table + the owner-confirmed nickname overlay (gitignored; resolution 95.7% weighted). Ground truth for "played" = Session Input's "Actual Attendees" (everyone, incl. non-roster free players). Final-list no-show = active entries on the last list before the 20:00 start that do not appear among actual attendees; any-point no-show = names appearing on any repost (including later struck-through) that never played — the definition comparable to RSVP-decay benchmarks; walk-on = played, never on the final list. Struck-through entries are cancellations, not no-shows, under the final-list definition. Comparison keys are person-ids where resolved, normalised strings otherwise. Headline set = dated Villingili lists (the 20:00 start is ground truth; other venues' cutoffs are [ASSUMPTION] and excluded). The cascade chart uses every repost of every dated list (points capped at 72h before start). Membership split: final-list entries joined to member_2025/2026 flags by session year (2024 excluded — membership didn't exist); unresolved raw-name entries count as non-members, which if anything inflates the non-member no-show rate — the null result is conservative. Personal thresholds: median 1-based position on the final list over ≥5 appearances (the numbered list is chronological); early-vs-late attendance rates are descriptive (position and reliability are both traits of the same regulars — no causal claim). All aggregates; no names in this page or its data payload. Script analysis/gP_s2.py.
