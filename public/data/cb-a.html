Denominator label — this page counts the never-joined periphery. Re-analysed in full on the presence substrate (2026-07-11): of the 150 who played but never joined the roster, 83% are purely peripheral (a single active month, then gone) and ~87% played exactly once — essentially the same funnel the first-timer log gave (~84%), confirming the old figure was never a measurement artifact. The other headline, ~50% one-and-done (PB-C2), uses a different denominator: all newcomers, including those who joined the roster and became regulars (median ~7 plays each). Both are correct at their own denominator; never quote one as the other.
Findings
  • 124 of 150 (83%) are purely peripheral — a single month of activity and gone. Wenger's "legitimate peripheral participation" here mostly never deepens.
  • A durable core of 25 people (12 insider + 13 boundary) sustains real engagement without ever joining the paying roster — the club's uncounted regulars.
  • Inbound (building-up) is almost empty: 0, and outbound 1 — few peripheral players are visibly moving toward or away from the centre; the pattern is touch-and-go, not a pipeline.
  • The monthly footprint confirms it: the periphery is a rolling churn of one-off appearances, not an accumulating cohort — a recruitment funnel that leaks at the very first step.
Method, data & caveats
Sequence analysis of the 150 (engagement='player'). Monthly state sequences over 2024-01…2026-06: A (played that month, from the presence substrate gS_rsvp.presence() — Session Input "Actual Attendees" person×session, 99.4% resolution, with new_players fallback for the 4 people never on an attendee list), T (talk-only, from message linkage), _ (absent). Each sequence is assigned a Wenger trajectory from its length, time span and recency: insider = ≥4 active months over ≥6-month span; boundary = ≥2 intermittent months spanning a gap; inbound = ≥2 months still active in the last 6; outbound = ≥2 months then lapsed; peripheral = a single touch. Rendered as a stacked-bar funnel (no d3-sankey vendored, per the shared lib rule). [ASSUMPTION] trajectory thresholds are analyst-set (not optimal-matching clusters); [ASSUMPTION] unresolved attendee names keep stable raw keys — a small bias toward single-touch sequences. Descriptive; no names emitted. Script: analysis/gB_cba.py. [Interpretation limitation, internally verified] A portion of the never-joined periphery single-touch players are short-stay visitors — travelling players and novelty drop-ins who were never convertible — so the count of genuine conversion failures is smaller than the peripheral share implies. The adjustment falls on the count, not the rate (visitors leave after one play at about the periphery's base rate). The basis is a decision-grade internal owner-coding worksheet; no figure from it is published here.
