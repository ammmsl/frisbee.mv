Patronage, not cost-recovery — and new ≠ renewing. The fee is a deliberate AGM-set patronage price: a one-time 100 MVR registration (new members only) plus annual membership. 2025 = 700 (100 reg + 600; all members joined that year). 2026 membership = 250 for a renewing member; a new 2026 member pays 350 (100 reg + 250). Members also pay a surcharge (member 10 / non-member 25). Per-player field cost has risen (~18 → 34 → 46 MVR), so the fee isn't chasing cost down. We use break-even (fee ÷ the 15 MVR/session surcharge saving) only as a tangible-benefit floor, and read a below-floor member as a patron. The DB can't isolate fee receipts from session payments; fees are AGM-set (org-wide), attendance is the pickup slice. 2026 is Jan–Jun only (an ×2 annualised read is shown too). Small-n: 51 2025 / 56 2026 members. Descriptive, not causal.
The tangible-benefit floor
Do members actually attend enough?
Findings
  • The fee is patronage, not cost-recovery: per-player field cost rose (~18 → 34 → 46 MVR) while membership fell 700 → 250 — a price members pay to sustain the club. Judged as patronage the model is coherent by design.
  • New and renewing 2026 members face different prices: a renewing member pays 250 (recouped after 17 sessions of surcharge saving — 48% already cleared it in H1), while a new member pays 350 (100 one-time registration + 250; recouped after 23, 38% cleared). The one-time registration is a joining cost, not a recurring one.
  • The 2025 700 floor needed 47 sessions and only 20% cleared it — so most 2025 members were patrons on pure play-economics. Median member attendance is 26 (2025) and 16 (2026 H1); the 250 renewal price sits near the median member's floor.
  • So the right test isn't “does it pay for itself” but “is the patronage price legible and accepted” — and it is. A member who never plays isn't an anomaly under patronage: the league reconciliation surfaces exactly one paid, zero-attendance patron (correction #7).
